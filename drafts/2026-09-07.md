## LinkedIn

**Hook**
My site used to put an item on the front page the moment it was nearly sold out. I reverted all of it.

**Body**
No seller chose that. No moderator approved it. The front page was just whichever item on any venture's menu happened to be down to its last unit.

That is an alarm, not an advertisement. A shop's turn on the front page decided by how empty their stock count was, not by anything they said.

So I reverted it. No field, no badge, no per venture strip. Then I built the opposite.

Now a seller asks. They pick one of their own items, write the line, and submit. A moderator approves it before it goes live. One slot, above the shop window, shared by everyone.

I split that slot into a 20 minute rotation at first, fair across a week and useless to the seller whose turn landed at four in the morning. It is a slider now, changing every 15 seconds in the visitor's own browser, so every visitor sees every advert regardless of when they showed up.

One more thing broke before it shipped. A venture running six adverts was getting six times the airtime of a venture running one, because the rotation cycled per advert instead of per venture. The turn belongs to the venture now, and its own adverts take turns inside that.

**Close**
Reverting a feature you already shipped is a strange kind of admitting the premise was wrong. What is the thing you built that you would take back down?

## Newsletter

**Subject**
I reverted a feature I had already shipped

**Body**
Until this week, ventureadda's front page had a strip that promoted whatever item on any venture was down to its last unit. Nobody asked for it to be there. It just appeared, decided entirely by whoever's stock count happened to hit zero first.

That is the wrong premise for a front page. An alarm about low stock and an advertisement are not the same thing, and I had built the alarm and called it a feature. So I reverted all of it: the field sellers used to mark an item limited time, the badge, the whole derived strip. None of it survived.

What replaced it inverts every part of the old design. A seller picks one of their own items, writes a line and an optional offer, and submits it. A moderator has to approve it before it is visible to anyone. One slot on the front page, shared by every venture, tagged PROMOTED so it never reads as the site's own opinion.

Getting the rotation fair took two more passes. The first version split that slot into 20 minute buckets across the week, so the seller whose turn landed at four in the morning got the same exposure on paper and nobody in practice. Then I found a shop running six adverts was getting six times the airtime of a shop running one, because the rotation cycled per advert instead of per venture. It now cycles every 15 seconds in each visitor's own browser, and a venture's own adverts share its single turn instead of multiplying it.

**Close**
If you have ever shipped something and only later realized the premise itself was wrong, not just the execution, I would like to hear what it was.
