## What changed while you slept

**I found a bug that would have broken your first post.** The LinkedIn API
version I shipped as the default, `202508`, was sunset by LinkedIn on 17 August
2026. I had taken it from memory rather than from their docs. Every post you
sent would have been refused. It is `202608` now, checked against LinkedIn's
versioning page, and there is a test that fails if the default ever goes stale
again.

**The escaping turned out to be more important than I said.** I had documented
`LINKEDIN_ESCAPE=0` as the fix if you ever saw stray backslashes. That advice
was wrong. LinkedIn's little-text format *requires* the escaping, and an
unescaped bracket does not look odd, it **truncates the post at that point**.
Half your post publishes and looks deliberate. The switch is now documented as
an escape hatch for a future format change, not a preference.

**The real reason your drafts were flat.** This is the one worth knowing.

`capture.py` ran `git log --format=%s`. Subjects only. So the weekly routine
never saw a single commit body, and the bodies are where all your writing is:

> Most sellers here photograph nothing. They are running a shop out of a hostel
> room between classes, and forty items with forty blank tiles is the normal
> outcome.

None of that reached a draft. The model was handed a list of one-line subjects
and asked to write a post, so it wrote a changelog with adjectives. That was
never a voice problem. It was a missing-input problem.

Capture now reads bodies. On your real week the note goes from a bare list to
8,871 characters carrying 12 explained commits. The drafting prompt also now
says to write from the sharpest thing in the notes and keep your words where
you already said it well.
